A quantum-circuit policy
driving a cycloidal robot joint

A 6-qubit variational quantum circuit (72 trained angles) adds feed-forward torque on top of the motor controller's PD loop. The circuit is simulated on the FPGA fabric of a PYNQ-Z2; the 1 kHz control stack was measured on the board with an emulated motor controller, and on 2026-10-13 the real actuator was driven open-loop from the PYNQ over CAN. Everything on this page runs the same models we trained and measured.

01 · Hardware

The actuator, built from its real printable parts

The 3D model is assembled from the project's own STL files (housing, 16-pin ring, 15-lobe cycloidal disc, eccentric input shaft, output carrier). Drag to orbit, explode it, or cut the housing open. The motion is kinematically correct: the motor turns 15 times for every output turn, and the disc wobbles on a 1.25 mm eccentric while it slowly rolls around the ring pins.

02 · Architecture

Residual control: the firmware stabilises, the circuit anticipates

The motor controller keeps its own position loop. Every millisecond the PYNQ-Z2 sends the target angle and a feed-forward torque τff in one CAN frame (Recoil RPDO3) and reads back position and torque. A wrong policy output can only degrade tracking — it cannot destabilise the joint.

04 · Inside the circuit

Six qubits, 64 amplitudes, one number out

Each control tick, six features of the reference and the tracking state are squashed into rotation angles xq = π·tanh(sq·fq) and re-uploaded in every layer. The trained rotations RX·RY·RZ are fused into one 2×2 matrix per qubit and layer, a CNOT ring entangles neighbours, and the expectation of Z on qubit 0 becomes τff = 0.8·tanh(2⟨Z₀⟩). 05 · FPGA

From 15,343 to 2,248 clock cycles

The state-vector simulator was written once in C and compiled both for the ARM cores and, with Vitis HLS, into a PL core. Five iterations took it from a design that could not meet 100 MHz to one that finishes in 22.5 µs. The decisive change was updating the state out-of-place (ping-pong buffers), which let every butterfly start one clock after the previous one.

Lesson from the bench. The first full PL core passed C/RTL co-simulation but returned wrong values on the board for mid-range tanh inputs. Moving tanh/cos/sin (13 scalars) to the ARM side and keeping only the 64-amplitude simulation in the fabric fixed it — and made the core smaller and faster.

06 · Results

The circuit works — and a 65-parameter MLP works better

All learned policies share the same inputs, output squashing and training (behaviour cloning followed by closed-loop back-propagation through time with domain randomisation), three seeds each.

Tracking error, randomised plants (256 episodes × 3 s)

Verdict

  • VQC helps: about 6× lower error than the firmware PD alone.
  • But no parameter advantage: an MLP with 65 weights has 32 % lower tracking error than the 72-angle circuit; a 4,673-weight MLP is no better than the small one.
  • The FPGA part delivers: 2.2× faster than the ARM core with deterministic timing.
  • No quantum advantage is claimed: six qubits are trivially simulable on a classical chip.

07 · Real hardware · 2026-10-13

First motion: the PYNQ drives the real actuator over CAN

The motor controller that died from soldering heat was replaced by a new B-G431B-ESC1 and brought up from scratch (ST-LINK firmware upgrade, option bytes, two-stage first flash). The test joint has no angle sensor (AS5600), and the stock firmware waits forever for one, so an open-loop build rotates the stator field to the commanded angle and the rotor follows like a stepper. This proves the whole command path — PYNQ → CAN → ESC → motor — but it is not a closed-loop experiment: the angles below are commands, not measurements.

Why the bus stayed silent at first. Four faults were stacked: the ESC's CAN pads needed a soldered header, one wire was loose, CANH/CANL were swapped, and the bus had no termination (driving PC14 high switches the ESC's 120 Ω on — inferred from the bus errors disappearing, not measured). A firmware bug then hid the fix: after the error burst two stale frames stayed in the receive FIFO and the upstream handler read one frame per interrupt, so every request was answered with the frame from two requests earlier. The handler now drains the FIFO.
